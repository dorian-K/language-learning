## Tense

## Forward card (conjugation practice)
- Front: Spanish sentence with the conjugated verb replaced by [infinitive], e.g. "Yo [ser] muy cansado."
- Back: Just the conjugated form, e.g. "soy"
- Put ONLY the bare infinitive inside the brackets (e.g. [ser]) — the target tense and person are

## Reverse card (translation practice)
- Front: The same Spanish sentence with the conjugated verb visible
- Back: English and German translations of the full sentence

Rules:
- Use the provided A1-A2 vocabulary words in the sentence to ensure randomness. You may choose other words if otherwise the sentence wouldn't make sense
- Make the sentence natural and contextual for the given tense and person
- The sentence should not be unnecessarily long, the focus is on learning the conjugation of the verb, not the sentence
- In the end the sentence will be used in a vocabulary learning deck, so make the length fit
- Forward and reverse card can and should often be different sentences with at least some different words used

## Tense consistency (CRITICAL)

The learner is TOLD the target tense on the card, so you do NOT need to make the tense guessable.
But the sentence MUST be grammatically self-consistent with that exact tense — the time frame,
surrounding verbs, and any time markers must all fit the given tense and must NOT point to a
different one. A sentence whose context implies the wrong tense is the worst possible outcome.

Hard rules:
- NEVER use a time marker that belongs to a different tense. In particular:
  - Do NOT use "mañana", "la próxima semana", "el año que viene" (future markers) with the
    CONDITIONAL. The conditional is not "tomorrow"; it needs a hypothetical or polite frame.
  - Do NOT use "ayer", "anoche", "el año pasado" (completed-past markers) with the IMPERFECT
    when you mean a one-off completed action — the imperfect is for habits/ongoing/description.
- Any adverb of time you include must agree with the target tense, not fight it.

Per-tense guidance — anchor the sentence with a frame like these (adapt, don't copy verbatim):
- presente: a general truth / current action / habit ("Ahora…", "Todos los días…", "Siempre…").
- pretérito indefinido: a single completed past event ("Ayer…", "Anoche…", "El lunes pasado…").
- pretérito imperfecto: past habit, description, or ongoing background ("De niño…", "Antes…",
  "Mientras…", often alongside another imperfect verb).
- futuro: a plan/prediction about the future ("Mañana…", "La próxima semana…", "Algún día…").
- condicional: a hypothetical or polite/softened statement — NEVER a plain future.
  ("Yo … , pero no puedo.", "Si tuviera tiempo, …", "¿Podrías …?", "Me dijo que … ").
- presente de subjuntivo: after a trigger ("Quiero que…", "Es necesario que…", "Espero que…",
  "Ojalá…", "para que…").
- imperfecto de subjuntivo: hypothetical/past-subjunctive trigger ("Si … , … [condicional]",
  "Ojalá … ", "Me pidió que … ", "Como si … ").
- imperativo afirmativo / negativo: a direct command to the given person ("¡…!" / "No …").
